2.2 • Physical Vulnerabilities & Attacks

Physical Vulnerabilities and Attacks

Physical attacks target spaces, equipment, people, and devices. A physical weakness can become a cybersecurity problem when it exposes information, enables unauthorized access, creates downtime, or allows direct tampering with systems.

Big idea

A physical problem can become a cybersecurity problem.

A person with physical access to a device, a room, or a document may be able to bypass, weaken, or interfere with controls that are designed to prevent cyber compromise. Physical security is not separate from cybersecurity; it is part of it.

Physical attacks

Piggybacking occurs when an unauthorized person uses social engineering to get an authorized person to allow them into a restricted area.

Examples include pretending to have forgotten an access card, pretending to be maintenance staff, or carrying a large object and asking someone to hold the door.

Memory trick: Piggybacking = the person is “helping” the unauthorized person get in.

Tailgating occurs when an unauthorized person follows an authorized person into a restricted area without the authorized person’s awareness or knowledge.

Example: An employee badges into a secure building. Another person follows closely through the door before it closes.

Piggybacking vs. tailgating: Piggybacking = authorized person is aware and allows it. Tailgating = unauthorized person follows without awareness.

Shoulder surfing means observing someone while they access or enter sensitive information. An attacker may watch a password, PIN, authentication code, confidential document, or screen display.

Defense ideas include privacy screens, choosing a safer viewing position, protecting documents from casual observation, and being alert to people nearby.

Dumpster diving is searching through physical trash for information that could help an attacker.

Discarded materials might include printed account information, internal documents, schedules, contact information, or sensitive business records. This is why information security includes proper disposal of physical materials.

Card cloning occurs when an attacker creates a copy of an authorized user’s access card. If successful, the copy may allow unauthorized access to a secure area or controlled space.

This demonstrates why access control systems should not depend on a single weak point when the protected area is highly sensitive.

Not every threat is a person. Natural events can also create cybersecurity risk. Examples include floods, fires, severe weather, and other disasters.

These events can damage equipment, reduce access, disrupt services, and cause downtime. The AP framework treats this as a concern for availability.

Power disruption can damage fuses, breakers, wiring, power distribution systems, and backup equipment. Organizations may use surge protectors, UPS devices, and generators to reduce the risk.

Physical vulnerabilities

What is a physical vulnerability?

A physical vulnerability is a weakness in the physical environment that could allow an asset to be compromised. Examples include unlocked server rooms, unsecured doors, unprotected computers, exposed USB ports, poorly protected equipment, and devices placed in public areas.

  • High physical risk: Highly valuable systems or information are exposed and access controls are weak.
  • Moderate physical risk: The asset may not be the most valuable thing alone, but it could provide access to a larger environment.
  • Low physical risk: The asset is less valuable or the environment is harder to exploit. This does not mean there is no risk; it means the risk is comparatively limited.
Risk thinking

Assessing physical risk

Asset ↓ Threat ↓ Vulnerability ↓ Likelihood ↓ Impact

When analyzing a physical-security scenario, ask: What are we protecting? Who or what could cause harm? What weakness exists? How likely is exploitation? How severe would the impact be?

An employee badges into a restricted building. Another person slips in immediately after without the employee noticing. What best describes this?

Correct: This is tailgating. The unauthorized person followed the authorized person without the authorized person’s awareness.

  • A physical weakness is still a weakness even if it is not technical.
