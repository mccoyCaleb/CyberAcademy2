Security Foundations Review

This section brings together the major ideas of Unit 1: attacker types, security practices, defense terms, and the importance of access control. These concepts work together to protect people, systems, and data.

Big idea

Cybersecurity is about layered protection.

People are often the first line of defense, but they can also be the weakest link. Systems need strong authentication, well-designed access controls, secure habits, and awareness of attacker tactics. Security is strongest when multiple controls work together.

1.1

Social engineering

Attackers manipulate trust, fear, curiosity, or urgency to trick people into taking harmful actions.

1.2

Password attacks

Passwords are still an important barrier, but weak or reused passwords can be exploited through brute force, spraying, stuffing, and other methods.

1.3

Layered defense

Security works best when organizations combine training, access restrictions, MFA, monitoring, and careful network practices.

The core themes of Unit 1

Attackers do not always break through the technology. Sometimes they exploit people by manipulating trust or creating urgency. This is why awareness and verification are critical parts of cybersecurity.

Even if one control fails, the next control may stop the attack or limit damage. This is the reason organizations use layered security and least privilege.

Confidentiality protects secrets, integrity protects accuracy, and availability keeps systems usable. Security teams balance these goals to defend an organization effectively.

Authentication verifies who someone is, authorization decides what they may do, and accountability ensures actions can be traced to a person or process. These ideas are essential in any secure system.

Real-world example

A student receives a message that appears to come from the school district and asks them to reset a password immediately. The message is a phishing attempt. A strong defense combines user awareness, secure login practices, MFA, verification steps, and monitoring tools so the attack is caught before the student enters credentials.

Which idea best explains why a password alone is not always enough protection?

Correct: It is defense in depth. A single control, such as a password, can fail, but multiple layers create more secure protection.
